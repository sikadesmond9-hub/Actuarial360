About the Actuarial Science Program

The origin, the mission, and what it truly means to study Actuarial Science at the University of Cape Coast.

What is Actuarial Science?

Actuarial Science is a discipline that applies mathematical and statistical methods to assess risk in insurance, finance, pensions, and other industries. Actuaries are the professionals who answer questions like:

  • How much should an insurance company charge for a policy?
  • How likely is a group of people to live to a certain age?
  • How should a pension fund invest to remain solvent for 40 years?
  • What reserves must a company hold to cover future claims?

In short: actuaries price risk. They are the silent architects behind insurance, banking, and long-term financial planning worldwide.

Origin & History

Where actuarial science began — and how it reached UCC.

Ancient Roots

Risk-Sharing Begins

The concept of spreading risk dates back to ancient civilisations — Babylonian traders pooling goods, Roman burial societies, and medieval guilds protecting members.

1662

John Graunt & the First Life Table

English statistician John Graunt published the first mortality table in London, laying the foundation for life insurance and demography.

1693

Edmond Halley's Life Table

Astronomer Edmond Halley — famous for Halley's Comet — produced the first scientifically rigorous life table, allowing insurers to price premiums fairly.

1848

The Institute of Actuaries Founded

In London, the Institute of Actuaries (now IFoA) was formed — the first body to formalise the profession and set examination standards.

Modern Era

Actuarial Science Reaches Africa

As African economies grew in the 20th and 21st centuries, universities began offering rigorous mathematical training to support the growing insurance, banking, and pension sectors across the continent.

Today

Actuarial Science at UCC

The University of Cape Coast offers Actuarial Science within the Department of Mathematics, equipping students with a strong foundation in probability, statistics, financial mathematics, and computing. Graduates go on to work in insurance, banking, pensions, consulting, and pursue international actuarial exams (IFoA, SOA).
